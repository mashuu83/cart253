## Reflective Journal Week 2

*September 23, 2026*

This week I used the three prototypes to try and explore a different aspect of p5, inspired by some of the examples on the site.

For my first experiment, I wanted to play with 2D objects rotating slowly in 3D space, to create a meditative kind of experience where the different rotation speeds of the shapes would end up intersecting each other in interesting ways. This prototype defintiely had the most learning, as I was initally quite confused as to why my shapes were not showing up where I expected. I realized that when choosing the WEBGL renderer, you are now in a "3D" mode, and the origin becomes the centre of the screen instead of the top left. Realizing this helped a great deal with placing my objects. I found myself sitting and watching this one a lot, I find something very soothing about the very slow rotations and simple composition.

The second experiment, I wanted to see if I could animate a ball moving back and forth in a jugglers hands, as one of the projects I would like to develop is a simple incrememental game, and one of the ideas for this I have is based on juggling. Even though the final prototype may look really basic, I am actually quite proud of what I accomplished here. I was able to create the look I wanted (A single ball going back and forth) by drawing 2 balls moving in opposite directions which are then hidden behind a rectangle below a certain point. Even though it may not look like much, I am really happy with how it turned out, and excited to iterate further. My next step would be to try and create the movement without "cheating" i.e. getting the movement to stop and reverse at certain points instead of just being constant circles.

Finally, I wanted to play with randomness and arrays, I started with an example on p5 and modified it from drawing 10 cubes to 5000. I added another paramater for color that was not in the original example, as well as commenting each section to ensure I understood what the code was doing at each step, and not just blindly copying example code. I enjoy the final color scheme I chose for the composition and I love how much the overall composition changes each time you refresh the page. I think I would like to add an automatic timer that refreshes the page after a set duration to create a more fleeting ephemeral type of illustation with each refresh. I like the final cube count I settled on because it fills the canvas while still generally having small patches of the black background visible.
